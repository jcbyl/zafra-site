What Is Zafra?

Zafra is the Spanish word for harvest season — the time when coffee cherries, cacao pods, and vanilla beans are picked and brought down from the mountains. It's a word that carries the weight of an entire agricultural year: the labor, the waiting, and finally, the gathering.

Zafra Trading Co. is the international distribution arm for Puerto Rican specialty agriculture. We source directly from farmer-owned cooperatives in Puerto Rico's central highlands — Yauco, Jayuya, Adjuntas, Maricao — and bring their harvest to markets that value what the island produces: coffee, cacao, honey, and vanilla.

We are not a middleman. We are the bridge. The co-op owns its processing. The farmers set their price. Zafra carries it to the world — and the world pays what quality deserves.

Direct trade. Farmers receive above-market pricing — not commodity floor rates. Price transparency is the foundation. Every buyer knows what the farmer was paid.

What We Carry

Puerto Rico grows some of the world's most distinctive agricultural products — island-grown, mountain-shaded, hand-harvested. Limited production, not commodity volume. These are products with a story, and a price that reflects it.

Coffee

100% Puerto Rican Arabica. Bourbon, Typica, Caturra, and USDA-TARS rust-resistant hybrids. Washed process. Regions: Yauco, Jayuya, Adjuntas, Maricao. SCA 82-88. Roasted to spec or green.

Cacao

Heritage Puerto Rican cacao, fine-flavor Trinitario varieties. Fermented and sun-dried to spec. Single-origin beans for chocolate makers and craft processors.

Honey

Mountain wildflower and coffee-blossom honey from PR's Cordillera Central. Raw, unfiltered, single-apiary. Pollinator health is part of the mission — not a byproduct.

Vanilla

Puerto Rican vanilla — cured and graded. One of the rarest vanillas in the world. From the Cooperativa de Vainilla de Puerto Rico and partner farms.

Additional products available through partner relationships: rum, brown sugar, molasses, and sugar derivatives via the Yabucoa Rum Company.

How It Works

The model is simple and transparent. Three independent entities, each doing one thing well:

1. Farmers Grow, harvest, and deliver cherry/pods/beans to the co-op at above-market prices.
→
2. Cooperative Processes, grades, and packages. Co-op owns the equipment. Farmers own the co-op.
→
3. Zafra Buys from the co-op. Ships to international buyers. Carries the story.

The farmer is not a supplier. The farmer is a partner. The cooperative is not a vendor. It's a member-owned enterprise. Zafra is not a middleman — it's the distribution channel that gets PR agriculture to the people who want it and pays the people who grow it.

Price Transparency

Every shipment carries a provenance card: region, farm or co-op, harvest date, processing method, and the price paid to the farmer. Buyers know the story. Farmers know their product is respected. That's the moat.

The Ecosystem

Zafra Trading Co. is the distribution layer of a three-entity agricultural platform built in Puerto Rico:

  • Fundación Areito de Puerto Rico — Non-profit umbrella. Programs: Instituto de Café (coffee education and quality), Polinizadores (pollinator science and beekeeping), Raíces Vivas (cacao genetics and fermentation), Propagación (tissue culture and nursery). Funds the infrastructure. Does not sell product.
  • Areito Cooperative — Farmer-owned. Owns processing equipment (grant-funded through the Foundation). Processes coffee, cacao, honey. Retains on-island distribution. Sells to Zafra for international export.
  • Zafra Trading Co. — International distribution. Buys from the co-op. Ships to US and international markets. B2B wholesale and consumer-facing retail.

These are independent entities with different owners. The cooperative is farmer-owned. The foundation is an independent non-profit. Zafra is the commercial arm.

Why Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico was once one of the world's great coffee origins. In the 1860s through 1890s, coffee was the island's #1 export. Then came industrialization, then Hurricane Maria (2017), which devastated 80% of the island's coffee trees.

Today, PR produces roughly 36,000 quintales of coffee a year but consumes 200,000. The gap is filled by imports. Domestic production is limited — and that limitation is also the value. PR coffee is among the rarest in the world. Island-grown, mountain-shaded, hand-picked.

The same story holds across the portfolio: PR cacao is heritage Trinitario, not commodity bulk. PR honey comes from mountain wildflowers in one of the most biodiverse islands in the Caribbean. PR vanilla is being revived by a cooperative that believes in it.

Zafra exists because these products deserve a channel that pays what they're worth — not what the commodity floor dictates.

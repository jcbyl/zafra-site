Coffee · Yauco · Jayuya · Adjuntas · Maricao

Coffee

Coffee was Puerto Rico's #1 export from the 1860s through the 1890s. The island's central highlands produce some of the rarest Arabica in the world — grown at 400-1,000m, hand-picked, washed processed.

Hurricane Maria (2017) devastated 80% of the island's coffee trees. PR produces roughly 36,000 quintales but consumes 200,000. That scarcity is the value.

$25/almud (28 lbs cherry) — what our cooperatives pay farmers. Government floor: $18. Market rate: $20.
Cacao · Heritage Trinitario

Cacao

Puerto Rican cacao is heritage Trinitario — the same Caribbean variety prized from Trinidad, Venezuela, and Madagascar. A natural hybrid of Criollo and Forastero.

PR's trees are descendants of Spanish colonial introductions, isolated for generations. Fine-flavor, single-origin, fermented to spec.

The Raíces Vivas program at Fundación Areito works on cacao genetics, fermentation, and quality training.
Honey · Cordillera Central · Africanized Bees

The Bee Story

In 1994, Africanized honey bees arrived in Puerto Rico. On the mainland, they're feared — aggressive, hard to manage. But on the island, something unexpected happened.

The population developed natural resistance to Varroa mites, the parasite devastating colonies worldwide. No miticide treatments. No chemical exposure. The bees are also notably gentle — defensiveness bred out over generations of island isolation.

Island isolation means PR honey can be certified organic. Bees on the mainland can't be, because they forage across property lines where pesticides are used. In Puerto Rico, there's nowhere for bees to reach contaminated fields.

The Polinizadores program at Fundación Areito runs queen bee breeding and pollinator health alongside production.
Vanilla · Castañer · USDA-TARS · Since 1909

Vanilla

The USDA's Federal Experiment Station (now TARS) in Mayagüez introduced commercial vanilla varieties in 1909. By the 1930s, it was a major research center for propagation, pollination, and curing.

A 1946 cooperative unified curing and grading. Then Operation Bootstrap pulled the island toward industry and vanilla faded.

Now it's coming back. The Cooperativa de Vainilla de Puerto Rico, founded in 2023 with 35 members, is reviving the tradition. Zafra is their channel to the international market — where vanilla is the second most expensive spice in the world, and PR-grown is nearly unheard of.

The Ecosystem

  • Fundación Areito de Puerto Rico — Non-profit umbrella. Programs: Instituto de Café, Polinizadores, Raíces Vivas, Propagación. Funds infrastructure. Does not sell product.
  • Areito Cooperative — Farmer-owned. Owns processing equipment. Processes coffee, cacao, honey. Retains on-island distribution. Sells to Zafra for export.
  • Zafra Trading Co. — International distribution. Buys from the co-op. Ships to US and international markets. B2B wholesale and consumer retail.
